# Slop filter

Every piece of copy passes this filter before the author sees it: LinkedIn and X posts, newsletters, X Articles, captions, video descriptions, outbound messages and the text on graphics. Run it on the whole draft, not only on the lines someone flagged. A flagged line is usually 1 instance of a pattern that appears elsewhere in the same draft.

## How to run it

1. Read the draft sentence by sentence against the 9 checks below.
2. For each hit, cut the line, or replace it with a plain fact or the author's own words (their posts, calls or chat). Never patch a slop line with a new slogan.
3. Read once more for repetition across sections: the intro, the item titles and the closing lines.
4. In the hand-off, say the filter ran and name the lines it cut.

## The 9 checks

| # | Pattern | Example | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Setup and transformation one-liners | "So this time we track where every signup comes from." | State the fact. Cut the "So..." line |
| 2 | Saying it twice | An intro line that announces the result right before the result; an item title its body repeats; a closing lesson that restates the intro | Say each fact once, where it does the most work |
| 3 | Process trivia | Internal rules, check intervals, warm-up durations | Ask: would the reader do anything differently knowing it? If not, cut |
| 4 | Stat stacks | "50% accept, 25% reply, 40% of replies are positive." | At most 1 number per point, the one that carries it |
| 5 | Slogans and rhetorical devices | "Everyone is doing X. Almost nobody says Y."; "It isn't X. It's Y."; 2-beat aphorisms; triads of fragments; colon reveals; "the real win is"; "here's the thing" | 1 plain sentence that says what happens |
| 6 | Labels, filler headings and stock words | Bold-label bullets, "game changer", "unlock", "seamless", "leverages", "Bonus:", "Here's the whole system" | The plain verb and the real thing by name |
| 7 | Explainer prose and spec sheets | "Here is the system behind it", a benefit sentence after every item | The author's own experience in connected sentences; the mechanism in 2 short examples |
| 8 | Pick-me and hardship flexes | "I thought working 14h days as the underdog would feel like misery." | A plain, honest admission |
| 9 | Closing questions for replies | "Which one would you push first?" at the end of an X post or a DM | End on the last fact, step or verdict. Ask only a specific question the author actually wants answered |

Two standing rules sit next to the checks: no "hit reply" call to action in newsletters, and text on graphics starts with a verb and names the real thing.
